## 1. David — "The Shepherd Who Won't Look the Part"

### Refined Description

- **Age/Build:** Mid-teens Middle Eastern youth (~15–16), lean and wiry, athletic but slight — clearly not yet a grown soldier. Shepherd-fit, not gym-built.
- **Skin:** Sun-bronzed olive with a warm ruddy flush across the cheeks and forearms (outdoor, weathered-healthy — this is the biblical "ruddy")
- **Hair:** Reddish-auburn brown, thick loose shoulder-length waves/curls, a little windblown and dusty from the field
- **Face:** Youthful open face, smooth jaw, **clean or only the faintest soft downy stubble**, strong brows, and **bright striking hazel eyes** (the "beautiful eyes" — keep them large and catch-lit)
- **Baseline expression:** Calm, quietly confident, alert — steady rather than fierce
- **Wardrobe:** Simple knee-length undyed wool/linen shepherd's tunic (natural off-white/tan, a little travel-dirtied at the hem), wide worn leather belt, a **rough sheepskin/fleece mantle slung over one shoulder**, plain leather sandals, a thin leather cord necklace
- **Off-sheet (separate props):** Leather sling, wooden shepherd's staff, small leather stone pouch, lyre/harp

- [ ] Face view large, sharp, bright hazel eyes clearly the focal point
- [ ] Skin reads sun-bronzed with a ruddy flush — not pale, not grey
- [ ] Skin reads as real photographed skin — visible pores and fine texture, not waxy or plastic (reject CGI-smooth faces)
- [ ] Lighting soft and even — no harsh shadow slicing the face, no hot glare blowing out the hair
- [ ] Full outfit visible tunic-to-sandals, nothing cropped; sheepskin mantle present in front and back views
- [ ] **No weapons, no sling, no staff, no armor** anywhere on the sheet — reject if the model adds them
- [ ] Background solid mid-gray, no gradients, no field/scenery sneaking in

## 2. Goliath (Gulayat) — "The Champion Who Fills the Frame"

### Refined Description

- **Age/Build:** Middle-aged Philistine champion; colossal, overwhelmingly muscular and broad. Giant proportions — a heroic ~8.5-head build with a proportionally large head, and **roughly twice the shoulder width of an ordinary man**. Mass, not just height, is what sells the giant.
- **Skin:** Deeply sun-bronzed, weathered, sweat-sheened, with scars — a lifetime outdoor warrior.
- **Hair/Beard:** Dark hair slicked back, a few loose strands; a **very full, long dark beard** reaching the chest.
- **Face:** Heavy furrowed brow, deep-set intense eyes, broad nose, grim/contemptuous baseline (he mocks before he fights).
- **Wardrobe (worn armor — stays on the sheet):** Coppery-**bronze overlapping fish-scale lamellar cuirass** over a dark tunic; ornate bronze **pauldrons with a raised central boss and rope-twist trim**; engraved bronze **vambraces** on bare massive forearms; a heavy leather war-kilt with bronze-studded straps (pteruges); **bronze greaves** on the shins; heavy leather sandals. Battle-worn, dented, oiled metal.
- **Kit inset:** his **domed bronze war helmet** (the one that flies off when the stone strikes) — locked as a 4th panel.
- **Off-sheet (separate prop assets):** spear (`@goliath_spear`), tower shield (`@goliath_shield`, carried by the shield-bearer), sword (`@goliath_sword`).

## Cast Scale Lineup — locks relative size (do this once, early)

The single most important asset for this storyline's size gags. Because each character sheet fills its own frame, the sheets **cannot** encode that Goliath dwarfs David or that Saul stands a head above his soldiers. This lineup fixes their relative size in one image — and it then becomes the yardstick every two-shot is checked against. Numbers come from `crs_table_Goliath_Complex.md` (the Scale Bible); base unit = average soldier ~160 cm = ×1.00.

**How this stays consistent — the critical part.** Do **not** generate the lineup from a text description of each character: that invents new people who won't match your finalized sheets. Instead, generate the individual identity sheets **first** (David, Goliath, Saul, an average soldier), then build the lineup by **feeding those finished sheets in as references**. Identity comes from the reference images; the text below imposes **only** relative size. So the sequence is: sheets → lineup (from sheets) → two-shots (from sheets + lineup). Requires an image tool that accepts multiple reference images (you confirmed yours does).

### Image-Generation Prompt (reference-driven)

Upload the four finalized sheets as references, then use this text. Keep identity description to a bare disambiguating minimum — the references carry the looks; the words carry the proportions.

```
UPLOAD (references, identity — do not restyle): @avg_soldier, @david, @saul, @goliath.

Character scale lineup — a TRUE-SCALE size chart, solid mid-gray studio background, even soft
lighting, photorealistic. Recreate the four referenced characters exactly as in their reference
images — same face, hair, skin, build, and outfit — changing ONLY their pose and their SIZE
RELATIVE TO EACH OTHER.
All four stand on ONE shared ground line, feet aligned on the same floor, facing camera, evenly
spaced left to right, each shown full-length head to toe. Camera far back on a long lens at the
average man's chest height, straight-on — no perspective foreshortening, no low heroic angle.

CRITICAL — this is a SCALE chart, so the height gap must be EXTREME and must not be flattened.
Do NOT normalize the four to similar heights, and do NOT resize each person to fill the frame.
Three of them are ordinary-sized men and the fourth is a colossal giant. Make the frame tall enough
that the giant fits at full height while the other three reach only up to his torso.

Left to right, obey these body-landmark locks exactly:
[1] Average soldier — ordinary height, the reference unit.
[2] David — a youth the SAME height as the average soldier; his youth shows in slight build and young
    face, NOT in height. David's crown is level with the soldier's crown.
[3] King Saul — one full head TALLER than the soldier and David; David's crown reaches only Saul's chin.
[4] Goliath — a COLOSSAL GIANT: nearly TWICE the total height of the other three and about twice their
    shoulder width, by far the tallest and most massive figure, his head towering near the top of the
    frame. HARD LOCKS: the average soldier's crown and David's crown reach only Goliath's NAVEL (a
    little above his waist); King Saul's crown reaches only Goliath's MID-CHEST. Goliath's head alone
    is far bigger than any other man's head.

Reading the crowns bottom-to-top: the soldier's and David's heads sit lowest and level with each other
at Goliath's navel; Saul's head one step higher at Goliath's mid-chest; Goliath's head far above all
three. All four keep their feet on the single shared ground line.
Neutral standing poses, arms relaxed. No props, no weapons, no scenery, no text labels.
```

If your tool binds references by upload order rather than name, drop the `@` tags and rely on the left-to-right slot order. The same reference-driven method builds the pairwise lineups below.

> **If the model keeps normalizing the heights** (rendering four similar-height men — Goliath as merely a big broad guy, David's crown up at his chest — the failure in the first batch): stop relying on the model to scale and **composite the lineup by hand**. Drop each accepted sheet onto one canvas at locked crown-height ratios measured from the same ground line — soldier = David = **1.00**, Saul = **1.16**, **Goliath = 1.86** — and give Goliath ~2× the shoulder width. That guarantees exact scale where a single generation is unreliable; then this lineup image becomes the yardstick every two-shot is checked against. Only keep a generated lineup if it already passes the landmark locks in QA.

### QA Checklist (accept/reject the lineup)

- [ ] **Heights NOT normalized** (the classic failure) — three ordinary men + one giant; if all four read as similar-height men and Goliath is just a bigger, broader guy, **reject immediately** and regenerate or composite. Beating this is the whole point of the sheet
- [ ] All four feet on ONE ground line — no figure "closer" or floating
- [ ] Measure pixel head-heights: David ≈ soldier; Saul ≈ 1.16× soldier; **Goliath ≈ 1.86× soldier (nearly twice their height)** — reject if Goliath isn't clearly the tallest by a huge margin
- [ ] David's crown lands at **Goliath's navel/waist** (not his chest); the average soldier's crown likewise; Saul's crown at **Goliath's mid-chest**
- [ ] Saul clearly a **full head** above David and the ordinary soldier
- [ ] Goliath reads **massive, not just tall** — ~2× shoulder width; reject a stretched thin giant
- [ ] David reads young via **build/face**, even though he's the same height as the soldier
- [ ] Background solid mid-gray, no scenery
